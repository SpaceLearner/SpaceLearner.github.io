01reason2 threads

Research

Most of my work comes back to one question. What does an agent need in order to finish a long task by itself?

It has to pick a sensible next step, carry it out with a tool or a robot arm, and get better from the feedback.

  1. IAgentic search and tool use

    Search is the first tool most agents need. The agent has to work out what to look for, read what comes back, and decide whether to search again.

    ZeroSearch trains this with reinforcement learning but swaps the real search engine for a simulated one, so training doesn't depend on live search calls.

    InterLV-Search asks what changes when images are part of the evidence, and whether an agent can use what it sees to decide where to search next.

  2. IIEmbodied agents: from perception to action

    A robot goes through the same cycle with a body. It looks around, makes a plan, moves, and sees what changed.

    RynnBrain does the looking and planning. It locates things in space and time from the robot's own point of view, reasons about the physical world, and writes task plans.

    WorldVLA and the RynnVLA series turn those plans into robot actions, and also predict what the scene will look like after each one.

In both threads I care most about agents that learn by trying things, not only from recorded demonstrations, and about how they juggle tools across many steps. The idea carries over to agents making decisions in real markets. Our generative auto-bidding work (KDD 2024) plans an advertiser's whole bidding trajectory in one go, and we tested it live on Alibaba's advertising platform. What I'm working toward is agents you can rely on for long tasks in messy, real environments.

Before agents, I worked on graph learning and recommender systems.

Selected work

IAgentic search and tool use

ZeroSearch

Corresponding author · 2025

ZeroSearch teaches LLM agents to search. Over several turns, the agent writes queries and reasons about what comes back. During RL training a simulated retriever plays the search engine, and we control the quality of the documents it returns, making them harder to use as training goes on.

Paper ↗Code ↗

InterLV-Search

Co-author · NeurIPS 2026, Evaluations and Datasets Track

A benchmark that checks whether agents use images to decide what to search for next, rather than only glancing at them when writing the final answer. It has three levels: going out to find visual evidence, searching in a controlled offline setting, and searching the open web where images and text are mixed.

Paper ↗Code ↗

IIEmbodied agents: from perception to action

RynnBrain

One of the project leads · 2026

An open embodied foundation model meant to act as a robot's brain. One model handles first-person perception, locating things in space and time, physical reasoning, and planning. I worked on pre-training, Chain-of-Point reasoning, VLA, and the training infrastructure.

Paper ↗Code ↗Contributions ↗RynnBrain 1.1 ↗

WorldVLA

Co-author · 2025

An action model with a world model inside it. The same autoregressive network outputs robot actions and predicts the video frames that follow, so the robot has some idea of what its actions will do.

WorldVLA came before RynnVLA-002, and its original repository now redirects there.

Paper ↗Code ↗Model ↗

RynnVLA series

Co-author · ICRA 2026 (001)

001 first learns from videos of people handling objects, filmed from their own point of view, then carries that over to robot control with trajectory-aware modeling and ActionVAE. Paper · Code · Overview

002 follows on from WorldVLA. It adds a continuous Action Transformer, a wrist camera, and the robot's own state, and models actions and future observations together. Paper · Code · Model

—Earlier work

AIGB / DiffBid

First author · KDD 2024

DiffBid looks at an advertiser's bids over a whole campaign as one trajectory and generates all of it with a conditional diffusion model. That avoids the errors that pile up step by step in Markovian RL. In an online A/B test on Alibaba's advertising platform, GMV went up 2.81% and ROI went up 3.36%. Paper

JGCF

A graph collaborative filtering method that uses Jacobi polynomials to shape the signals on the user–item graph. KDD 2023. Paper · Code
